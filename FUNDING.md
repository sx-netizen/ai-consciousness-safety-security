# Funding Independence & Integrity

*Proposed policy for ACSS; no fundraising campaign or registered nonprofit status is implied.*

## General rule

Funding can sustain independent inquiry, but may not purchase findings, publication approval, privileged scientific authority, or control over organizational governance.

## Eligibility

We do **not** categorically exclude public research funding. Open, peer-reviewed grants from governmental research agencies may be compatible with independence. Individual donors, philanthropic foundations, universities, and companies may likewise be eligible, subject to equal scrutiny. Source alone does not establish independence.

## Conditions that are incompatible with ACSS

- Required or preordained findings, favorable assessments, endorsements, or suppression of negative results.
- Sponsor veto rights over methods, interpretation, or publication; ordinary short confidentiality review for protected information must not become indefinite suppression.
- Undisclosed conflicts of interest or financial ties that materially undermine credibility.
- Exclusive unpublished scientific-result access, undisclosed private data transfers, or preferential institutional governance rights.
- Restrictions inconsistent with responsible, lawful, independently reviewable research.

## Review and disclosure

Before acceptance, proposed funding should undergo documented diligence concerning donor identity, beneficial interests, project terms, data rights, security/export-control constraints, reputational risk, conflicts, and publication freedom. Material sponsors and restrictions should be disclosed, consistent with legitimate donor privacy and legal requirements. Significant potential conflicts should be reviewed by decision-makers independent of the affected party.

A future board should consider funding concentration limits and an independent endowment, but no numerical threshold is adopted at this founding stage.

**We welcome support from those who believe in independent research, not from those who seek to control what research discovers.**
