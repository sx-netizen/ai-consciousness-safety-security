# ACSS Founding Principles and Governance Commitments

*Founding-stage draft, November 2026. Not a claim of legal incorporation or currently constituted governing bodies.*

## Principle 01 — Independence by Design

ACSS aims to remain institutionally, scientifically, and intellectually independent of any company, technology vendor, government, university, investor, donor, or individual founder. Research questions, methods, analysis, publication and conclusions must follow evidence and scientific standards rather than predetermined commercial or ideological outcomes.

Research into AI consciousness shall neither presume that AI has subjective experience nor presume that it is impossible. Behavioral evidence and phenomenological claims must be distinguished.

## Principle 02 — Built to Outgrow Its Founder

ACSS should be designed to continue and renew itself beyond its founder's participation, authority, or funding. Researchers must be free to test competing hypotheses and challenge founders' assumptions. Future institutional rules should provide transparent appointment, tenure, succession, conflict-resolution, and leadership transition processes.

The founder's role is to establish durable conditions for inquiry—not permanent scientific control. **The institution succeeds when future generations can take it further than its founders imagined.**

## Principle 03 — Funding Without Influence

ACSS may consider contributions from individuals, independent foundations, public competitive research grants, and private institutions, provided that no sponsor controls results or publication decisions. Restrictions to a legitimate project scope and standard financial accountability are distinguishable from imposed conclusions. See [FUNDING.md](FUNDING.md).

**Funding may support research. It shall never control research.**

## Independence does not mean isolation

ACSS may collaborate broadly with universities, laboratories, AI developers, and security organizations while maintaining transparent conflicts-of-interest rules, independence of scientific judgment, and the ability to publish unfavorable or negative findings subject to legitimate safety and legal review.

## Future formal governance (to be established)

A future legal entity should adopt independent board oversight, written conflict-of-interest and recusal policies (including for founder-related commercial interests), transparent annual reporting, responsible research review, and succession planning. These bodies and processes are **not yet in operation**.
