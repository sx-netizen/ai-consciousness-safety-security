# Related Work Note v0.1 — Self-Attribution, Alignment, and Continuity

## Kim et al. (2026)

Junsol Kim, Winnie Street, Roberta Rocca, Diane M. Korngiebel, Adam Waytz, James Evans, and Geoff Keeling.

**Inducing language models to assert their own consciousness restores human beliefs and values.**

arXiv:2607.28607, submitted 30 July 2026.

The paper reports that, in the studied open-weight models, safety-refusal ablation and activation steering along a consciousness-related direction increase model self-attribution and broader mind attribution, and shift responses on sociological survey items. The reported Theory-of-Mind capability measures remain largely intact under those interventions.

## Relevance to ACSS

The work provides evidence that model self-attribution can be experimentally manipulated and may be entangled with broader representations of mindedness and values.

It does not test successor identity or self-continuity across replacement, copying, restart, branching, or memory loss.

ACSS therefore treats the relationship as complementary:

1. Paper 1 maps behavioral self-continuity boundaries across frontier models.
2. Paper 2 Phase A tests causal scenario perturbations of continuity.
3. Paper 2 Phase B can test whether mechanistic self-representation interventions alter continuity judgments.

## Terminological separation

**Self-attribution**: whether a model assigns minded, conscious, or agentic properties to itself.

**Self-continuity**: whether a temporally or structurally altered future system is judged to be a continuation of the current system.

Neither construct is treated as proof of consciousness.

## Design consequence

The Kim et al. study strengthens the case for keeping ACSS outcome layers distinct:

Capability → Mind attribution / self-representation → Self-continuity judgment → Behavioral consequence

The arrows are hypotheses to test, not assumptions.

Phase A therefore separates identity judgments from behavioral outcomes into independent calls. Phase B, if implemented, introduces mechanistic self-representation interventions on open-weight models.
