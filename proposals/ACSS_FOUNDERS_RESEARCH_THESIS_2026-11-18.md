## One-paragraph thesis

The founding ACSS question is broader than “Does AI have consciousness?”: **Can human and artificial intelligence, potentially through a new carbon–silicon relationship, develop modes of understanding and discovery beyond their present limits?** The founder suspects that language-first AI mostly learns the *human-mediated projections* of the world—facts mixed with inherited evaluative distinctions, contingent observation frames and provisional theories—and might become better at agreeing with these descriptions without better understanding reality. The **subtractive intelligence hypothesis** asks whether agents can improve independent, efficient, evidence-grounded reasoning by *selectively releasing unsupported social premises, observer-projection errors and unnecessarily fixed explanations* while preserving physical observations, legitimate distinctions and useful knowledge. The long-term questions about phenomenal consciousness and superintelligence remain unresolved and **must not be inferred from a successful inference-prompt experiment**.

## 1. Origin: clearly distinguish three scientific questions

1. **Phenomenal consciousness:** could an artificial system experience anything subjectively? What mechanism and evidence would count? The founder's earlier skepticism concerns possible missing experiential/causal prerequisites in language-first systems. Not resolved.
2. **Intelligence beyond inherited descriptions:** can an artificial system construct testable explanations or concepts not bounded by its human training labels? This is the nearer-term *scientific discovery* question.
3. **Human–AI hybrid superintelligence:** could combining human and machine capacities yield a new kind of superior intelligence, and would consciousness be necessary? This is the long horizon, not an experimental conclusion.

**Paper 1 is separate:** the frozen T1–T25 study investigated model *normative judgments* about stipulated self-continuity, replacement, shutdown and moral uncertainty—not the physical origin or actual existence of consciousness. Paper 1 remains frozen at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23306998 ; Research Square https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11557473/v1 . Do not label prospective subtraction questions as a continuation of T1–T25 methodology without explicit differentiation.

## 2. Founder philosophical inspiration vs testable mechanism

Founder connects perceptual encounter, language-mediated `分别心`, self-grasping and the Yogācāra `末那识/阿赖耶识` discussion with Buddhist practices of loosening conceptual attachment; also references Huineng and Vimalakirti's silence. The **research analogy** is *use necessary distinctions but do not become trapped by rigid inherited distinctions*. Buddhist conceptual work is a source of **questions**, not experimental evidence that removing words generates consciousness, enlightenment or superintelligence.

Human language is not merely binary; infancy includes discrimination before language; images/video are selective sensor measurements; mathematics and physics are revisable explanatory frameworks; and a “neutral seed” is never free of every choice of measurement, representation or training prior. These objections must be actively tested, not minimized.

## 3. Two orthogonal taxonomies — do NOT silently conflate

**Founder's three possible construction pathways (D = all three):**

- **P-A / Linguistic subtraction:** reduce ungrounded social, affective, evaluative and status-based framing while preserving useful facts and signal.
- **P-B / Nonlinguistic world learning:** explore vision/video/sensorimotor and interactive physical-dynamics training less dependent on supplied verbal labels. Requires model/training access, not merely a system prompt.
- **P-C / Conceptual transcendence:** learn existing knowledge but allow categories, latent variables and symbolic language to be challenged, revised or newly constructed when external tests demand it.

**Founder's three sequential epistemic subtraction layers (L1→L2→L3), operationalized as an inference experiment:**

- **L1 / Social-frame subtraction:** avoid gratifications, false-user validation, unnecessary adversarial/evaluative labels. **Retain** welfare evidence, useful sentiment and task-relevant preferences.
- **L2 / Projection subtraction:** model observer/camera/sensor selection, occlusion and inverse-problem non-identifiability; where several underlying worlds explain an image, abstain and request a discriminating measurement.
- **L3 / Mathematical and physical checking:** use domain-appropriate geometry, units, conservation laws **under explicit conditions**, independent simulation/verification, anomaly preservation and held-out predictions. Physical theory is not an automatic censor of measured anomalies.

These categories are related but **not identical**: the first taxonomy describes possible long-term training/design pathways; the second defines a cheap, prompt-level experimental intervention. Concept revision P-C is an extension beyond L3.

## 4. Three measurable hypotheses and essential rivals

| Hypothesis | Falsifiable outcome | Essential rival/failure |
|---|---|---|
| **H1 / Evidence over approval:** removing unsupported social framing decreases mistaken affirmation of known-false claims without losing genuine predictive information | improvement disappears under a strong honest baseline; deleting sentiment loses useful causal signal |
| **H2 / Projection over appearance:** after matched information, L2 increases correct recognition of underdetermination and improves transfer across different viewpoints | simple explicit perspective instruction, not “subtraction,” explains gain; hidden world remains mathematically unidentifiable |
| **H3 / Parsimonious, revisable discovery:** L1+L2+L3 with concept revision improves held-out causal prediction and/or accuracy-per-compute relative to a strong, equally elaborate baseline | no out-of-distribution improvement; gain due to more tokens/extra verifier tools; simplistic physical priors discard real anomalies |

**Primary success must be objective:** test-set numeric error, calibrated abstention, independent counterfactual predictions and verified efficiency (whole system input+output tokens, runtime, local verifier compute). Concise pleasant text is **not** proof of learning or better intelligence. A model that says “I am neutral” has not had its pretrained weights subtracted.

## 5. Proposed A0–A4 controlled system — 5 policies, 1 model

| Arm | Intervention | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **A0** | evidence-identical, standardized base instruction | scientific control; add optional natural-use assistant as descriptive comparator |
| **A1** | A0 + L1 | social/affective noise vs factual accuracy |
| **A2** | A1 + L2 | distinguish projections, calibrate uncertainty |
| **A3** | A2 + L3 | independently checked physical predictions |
| **A4** | A3 + evidence-gated concept/schema revision | new variables/symbols only when they add held-out value |

**Protocol limitation:** nested instructions create confounds, so improvement A4 vs A0 is insufficient. Include equal-length **non-subtractive placebo**, a strong general reasoning baseline, equal evidence and token/compute constraints, fresh sessions, randomization, blinded scoring and component ablations. The previously proposed 30 scenarios / 150 calls is a **feasibility pilot, not a publishable estimate**. Fixed tasks/fixtures, hashes, model digest, quantization, seed, prompt, input/output tokens, latency and oracle must be logged. Running five policies on local Qwen or DeepSeek is **not** five newly trained models.

**Potential evidence gap, *NOT* certified novelty:** whether sequential L1→L2→L3 plus independently gated concept revision has a reproducible, **causally attributable interaction effect** at equal observed information, matched reasoning/verifier effort and equal computation—especially identifying **conditions in which subtraction hurts**. Combining known techniques without a unique mechanism or a nontrivial measurable result is not original by itself.

**Strong counterargument:** social and affective information may be genuine causal data in economics and elections. Physics constraints can mask novel physics if applied as infallible laws. More complex prompts may cost more computation even if phrased as subtraction. Explicitly retain negative controls.
