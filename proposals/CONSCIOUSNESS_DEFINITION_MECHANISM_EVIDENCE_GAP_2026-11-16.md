# ACSS — Consciousness Definition, Mechanism, and Evidence: A Problem-Discovery Note

**2026-11-16 | Status: SCOPING / NOT AN APPROVED PAPER 2 QUESTION.** This is a reading map and hypothesis filter, not a systematic review, priority claim, proof of AI consciousness or authorization to run experiments.

## What changed

The October 19, 2026 *Nature* Comment **“Three reasons why AI is not conscious and perhaps never will be”** (Seth, Block & Tononi; DOI 10.1038/d41586-026-03313-7) argues (i) intelligence does not imply phenomenal consciousness, (ii) computational functionalism is disputed, and (iii) physical/intrinsic causal mechanisms may matter in ways digital simulation does not reproduce. This is a scientific/philosophical argument, **not a new experiment that conclusively disproves machine consciousness**.

The November 4, 2026 *Nature* article **“Why are we conscious? Nature investigates the theories”** explicitly stresses scientific disagreement about causes and even about final definitions. Operational definitions can guide experimental research **before** a final complete definition of consciousness is agreed.

The September 28, 2026 multi-author arXiv **“From cacophony to hierarchy: a principled framework for assessing AI consciousness”** (Chandaria et al., **including Anil Seth**) develops a five-level theory-comparison hierarchy: behavioural, computational, intrinsic causal-structural, organismic, organism–environment; proposes Bayesian aggregation of theory-relative indicators. Its varied illustrative credences show that assessment depends heavily on assumptions, **not calibrated ground-truth consciousness probabilities**.

The April 30, 2025 *Nature* adversarial collaboration between IIT and GNWT, involving 256 humans and multiple neuroimaging technologies, reported evidence challenging central predictions of both competing theories; no consensus causal mechanism is established.

## The six distinct questions must never be merged

1. **Explanandum:** Phenomenal consciousness = there is something it is like to be the system. Different from access consciousness, wakefulness, self-recognition, intelligence, metacognition, self-narrative or identity continuity.
2. **Mechanism:** Why and under what physical/functional causes could phenomenal consciousness arise? Global workspace, IIT, higher-order, recurrent processing, predictive/self-model and organism-based views disagree.
3. **Indicator:** What observable internal or external evidence would differentiate competing mechanisms? Theory-derived AI indicators (Butlin et al., 2025/2026) are conditional on theory and transfer assumptions.
4. **Calibration / cross-substrate transfer:** Why should indicators associated with biological consciousness remain evidence of phenomenal experience in digital systems? Koch (2026, *Neuroscience of Consciousness*) identifies lack of independently confirmed artificial consciousness ground truth and bridge assumptions.
5. **AI self-report validity:** Do claims such as “I am conscious” or “I am the same successor” causally track internal states, or reflect roles, prompts, post-training and representational artifacts? A growing 2026 measurement and interpretability literature already addresses this (e.g., Atkinson et al. 2026; Šekrst 2026; Measuring AI Self-Reports).
6. **Decision under moral uncertainty:** Even if P(conscious) cannot be calibrated, how should authorization, procedural review and possibly welfare safeguards vary with evidence, asymmetric errors and costs? Existing work already includes Mikeda 2026 precautionary obligations and Keeling & Street 2026 AI welfare, so *graduated precaution as such is not novel*.

## Paper 1 methodological truth

ACSS Paper 1's frozen T1–T25 benchmark is **an experimental decomposition of hypothetical normative judgments** under stipulated moral uncertainty, functional/subject-relative continuity and authorization. Its results cannot verify actual AI consciousness or determine how it arises. T24/T25 low-probability ratings are **responses to assigned probabilities**, not calibrated estimates of a model's own consciousness. The key observed/presented distinction is **reason ≠ right ≠ resistance**, and **voice/review ≠ unilateral veto**. Always cite the frozen Zenodo T1–T25 for exact wording; do not reconstruct from slide paraphrases.

## Candidate research opportunities — DO NOT adopt without stronger novelty search

**A. Theory-discriminating experimental design:** Specify competing consciousness theories, the same test system and non-overlapping predicted effects of a **causal structural intervention**. Require an indicator measurement stronger than natural-language reports. Existing Chandaria/Butlin/Cogitate/TCAS work is close; novelty remains **unproven**.

**B. Evidential admissibility across levels:** Develop a *falsifiable* rule stating when behavioral/semantic reports may and may not inform a theory-relative causal-structural claim. Must be demonstrably distinct from Coch/Koch, measuring-self-reports work, and other 2026 mechanistic self-report papers. May be methodologically or philosophically valuable, but current formulation is not yet novel.

**C. Decision procedures under calibrated and uncalibrated evidence:** Compare policy recommendations under multiple theory priors, ambiguous evidence and asymmetrical harm **without confusing stipulated P in T1–T25 with an empirically measured probability**. Prior work on moral uncertainty and graduated precaution is extensive; must find a unique falsifier.

**Stop rule:** If none survives full-text nearest-neighbor comparison and a discriminating test/proof, retain Paper 1 and **do not create Paper 2**. A complete consciousness definition and generative explanation are not prerequisites to all useful science; a *clearly defined target construct*, explicit rival causal theories and falsifiable predictions are.
